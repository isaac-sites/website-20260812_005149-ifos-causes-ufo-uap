## Introduction

The illusion is especially persuasive because the reflection is genuinely present in the photograph. It is not necessarily digital manipulation, lens flare or an invented object: it is a second optical scene superimposed on the first. Research on photographing through glass describes precisely this separation problem, with a transmitted background and a reflected layer occupying the same pixels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/single-image-reflection-removal-through-cascaded-refinement/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: microsoft.com">[Microsoft]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">microsoft.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Single Image Reflection Removal through Cascaded Refinement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Single Image Reflection Removal through Cascaded Refinement - Microsoft ResearchJune 1, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2020</span></span></span>

## How car windows combine two scenes

Glass is transparent enough to reveal the road and landscape, but it is not perfectly non-reflective. Light from the vehicle interior can bounce from the glass into the camera while exterior light passes through it. The resulting photograph is effectively a composite created optically at the moment of exposure: one layer represents the scene beyond the window and another represents objects or lights on the camera's side of it. Computer-vision researchers regard separating those layers from a single photograph as an inherently difficult problem because both contribute information to the same image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/single-image-reflection-removal-through-cascaded-refinement/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: microsoft.com">[Microsoft]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">microsoft.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Single Image Reflection Removal through Cascaded Refinement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Single Image Reflection Removal through Cascaded Refinement - Microsoft ResearchJune 1, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2020</span></span></span>

That superposition explains one of the most deceptive features of false UFO photographs. A reflection can appear visually associated with the distant scenery even though it has no physical relationship to it. A reflected control may seem to hover just above a treeline; a bright cabin feature may apparently sit between clouds; a dashboard display can seem to form a group of coloured lights over a road. Nothing in a single two-dimensional photograph automatically tells the viewer that those pixels originated from light reflected centimetres away rather than from an object hundreds of metres or kilometres away.

Cars are particularly effective environments for producing the effect because their glass is surrounded by potential reflection sources. Dashboard surfaces are sufficiently reflective that automotive researchers have studied the visibility problems caused when their images are superimposed on the road scene through a windscreen. SAE research, for example, has measured [reflections]({{ 'reflections/' | relative_url }}) from instrument-panel materials under different windscreen, panel and illumination angles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://saemobilus.sae.org/articles/predicting-effects-veiling-glare-caused-instrument-panel-reflections-windshields" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: saemobilus.sae.org">[SAE Mobilus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">saemobilus.sae.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAE Mobilus2008-01-0666: Predicting Effects of Veiling Glare Caused by Instrument Panel Reflections in the Windshields - Journal ArticleA...</span></span></span> A General Motors service bulletin concerning the 1987 Buick LeSabre likewise documented instrument-cluster lights reflecting into the windscreen during night driving strongly enough that a revised bezel was specified as the remedy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://charm.li/Buick/1987/LeSabre%20Estate%20Wagon%20V8-307%205.0L/Repair%20and%20Diagnosis/Technical%20Service%20Bulletins/All%20Technical%20Service%20Bulletins/Instrument%20Cluster%20Lights%20-%20Reflect%20Onto%20Windshield/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: charm.li">[Charm]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">charm.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Instrument Cluster Lights</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Instrument Cluster Lights - Reflect Onto Windshield — 1987 Buick LeSabre Estate Wagon V8-307 5.0L Service Manual &#124; Operation CHARM...</span></span></span>

This is therefore not an exotic photographic mechanism invented to explain UFO pictures. Windscreen and window reflections are ordinary, measurable features of vehicle optics.

## Why an interior fitting can look like a distant craft

BUFORA investigator Philip Mantle's published examples make the problem unusually concrete. In demonstration photographs taken from inside a vehicle, an apparent object beyond the glass could be identified in a subsequent photograph as the reflection of the silver Peugeot emblem on the steering wheel. Another photograph showed an apparent UFO in the upper-right portion of the image while visible parts of the vehicle established that the photographer was inside a car; the object's shape closely resembled a reflected dashboard cup holder.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/reflecting-on-ufo-images" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">reflecting on ufo images</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">REFLECTING ON UFO IMAGESMarch 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

These examples illustrate why identifying the source is not always intuitive. The reflected object does not have to look like a complete, familiar car component. Illumination, viewing angle, focus and the reflectivity of the material can preserve only its brightest edges or highlights. A circular fitting may become an "orb"; a metallic badge can become a compact craft-like silhouette; several illuminated controls can become a formation of lights.

The camera can make that ambiguity worse. The outside landscape is normally what the photographer intends to focus upon, so a nearby reflected cabin feature may be soft or partially blurred. Conversely, a bright reflection against a dark exterior can remain conspicuous even when its physical source is difficult to recognise. Practical photography guidance specifically warns that reflections are a recurring problem when shooting through car, tram and train windows, and recommends blocking interior light around the camera when possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digital-photography-school.com/tips-for-minimizing-reflections-when-photographing-through-windows/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digital-photography-school.com">[Digital Photography School]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digital-photography-school.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Digital Photography SchoolHow to Prevent Reflections When Photographing Through WindowsMay 10, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 10, 2024</span></span></span>

A reflection can also be mistaken for something positioned *behind* foreground scenery simply because human vision interprets the photograph as one coherent three-dimensional scene. Unless a tree branch, sign or other genuine exterior object visibly occludes the alleged UFO, its apparent [distance]({{ 'distance/' | relative_url }}) is largely an inference.

## Seat-belt fittings, badges and controls are useful suspects

Car-window cases reward attention to small pieces of interior hardware. Dixon told *The Guardian* that UFO investigations can turn on surprisingly mundane details, including a seat-belt buckle reflected in a car window.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2023/feb/16/ufo-chinese-spy-balloon-mystery" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Guardian Most UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">But what about the other 2%?Heather Dixon, head of national investigations for the British UFO Research Association (Bufora), discusses t...</span></span></span> BUFORA's later illustrated treatment broadens the lesson: steering-wheel badges, cup holders and belt hardware can all generate shapes that become misleading once isolated from their automotive context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/reflecting-on-ufo-images" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">reflecting on ufo images</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">REFLECTING ON UFO IMAGESMarch 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

Several characteristics make these components worth checking first:

* **Metallic and glossy fittings produce strong highlights.** Chrome-effect trim, badges and polished seat-belt hardware may remain visible as reflections even when darker parts of the cabin disappear.
* **Controls already have suggestive geometry.** Rings, rectangles, illuminated symbols and groups of buttons can resemble structured craft or [formations]({{ 'formations/' | relative_url }}) when reflected without their surrounding dashboard.
* **Displays supply coloured lights.** Instrument clusters, infotainment screens and other illuminated controls provide exactly the bright, compact sources most conspicuous against a dark exterior.
* **Only part of the source may appear.** The reflected image can be cropped by the window, softened by focus or reduced to bright edges, making straightforward visual recognition harder.
* **The apparent object may never have been noticed during the sighting.** A shape discovered only after reviewing photographs deserves particularly careful examination for a window reflection or other photographic artefact. BUFORA's examples include supposed objects that were not observed directly when the photograph was taken.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/reflecting-on-ufo-images" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">reflecting on ufo images</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">REFLECTING ON UFO IMAGESMarch 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

The important comparison is therefore not merely "Does this look like a UFO?" but "What inside this particular vehicle has the same arrangement of bright edges, colours and shapes?"

## Motion can expose the reflected layer

A sequence of photographs or video is usually more informative than a single frame. Researchers working on computational removal of window reflections exploit a fundamental clue: as a camera moves, the reflected and transmitted scenes can exhibit different apparent motion. That difference can help separate the two layers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01180" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Learning to See Through Obstructions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv Learning to See Through Obstructions</span></span></span>

The same principle is useful without specialised software. If the phone shifts relative to the window, a nearby reflected object changes position according to the camera-window-cabin geometry rather than behaving like an object fixed in the distant landscape. BUFORA recommends moving the observer's head from side to side when checking suspected window reflections; a reflected object will respond to that change in viewpoint.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/reflecting-on-ufo-images" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">reflecting on ufo images</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">REFLECTING ON UFO IMAGESMarch 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

Video can reveal even stronger contradictions. In a widely discussed 2024 clip described as showing a cylindrical object over Beijing, an analysis on Metabunk argued that the apparent object was a car-window reflection. The interpretation was based not simply on resemblance but on relationships within the sequence: orange flashes corresponded with passing streetlights, a green component appeared around the time the vehicle approached an illuminated green sign, and the supposed object appeared in front of a street sign in the image rather than behaving as a distant object behind it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/bioluminescent-ufo-over-beijing-window-reflection.13748/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bioluminescent UFO over Beijing (Window Reflection) &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bioluminescent UFO over Beijing (Window Reflection) &#124; Metabunk</span></span></span>

That example should not be treated as a universal rule that moving UFO videos from cars are reflections. It shows the stronger analytical method: test whether the object's behaviour follows the vehicle, window and nearby light sources better than it follows the external landscape.

## Practical checks for a vehicle-window UFO photograph

A convincing reflection diagnosis should explain specific features of the image rather than merely invoke "glass" as a possibility. The strongest investigations combine several independent clues.

**Establish whether glass was in front of the camera.** Look for the windscreen edge, door pillar, dashboard, window tint, smears or other reflected cabin details. If the photographer remembers the circumstances, ask whether the window was raised or lowered. A photograph taken outside the vehicle obviously removes this particular explanation.

**Ask what was visible to the naked eye.** If the alleged craft appeared only when the photograph was examined later, an optical artefact deserves greater attention. This does not prove reflection, but it removes the important evidence of an observer independently seeing the object in the exterior scene. BUFORA explicitly flags this distinction in its photographic examples.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/reflecting-on-ufo-images" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">reflecting on ufo images</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">REFLECTING ON UFO IMAGESMarch 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

**Inspect the whole photograph, not just the "UFO".** Investigators should search the dashboard, steering wheel, centre console, door trim and seat-belt hardware for matching shapes. Cropping can hide exactly the contextual clues needed for identification, so the original uncropped image is much more valuable than a social-media enlargement of the object alone.

**Compare neighbouring frames.** A second photograph taken after the camera has moved may expose the reflected source or change its apparent position. BUFORA's Peugeot demonstration becomes readily understandable when the sequence reveals the steering-wheel emblem responsible for the supposed aerial object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/reflecting-on-ufo-images" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">reflecting on ufo images</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">REFLECTING ON UFO IMAGESMarch 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span>

**Look for impossible layering.** If the alleged distant object visibly overlays an exterior sign, window frame or other feature that should be nearer to the camera, reflection becomes a strong candidate. The Beijing video analysis used this kind of spatial contradiction as a central clue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/bioluminescent-ufo-over-beijing-window-reflection.13748/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bioluminescent UFO over Beijing (Window Reflection) &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bioluminescent UFO over Beijing (Window Reflection) &#124; Metabunk</span></span></span>

**Recreate the geometry where possible.** Using the same vehicle, seating position and approximate camera angle can be much more persuasive than arguing from appearance alone. Illuminate or expose suspected controls and move the camera until their reflections occupy the same portion of the glass. Successful [reconstruction]({{ 'reconstruction/' | relative_url }}) can account simultaneously for shape, orientation, colour and location.

## What counts as strong evidence of a reflection?

Shape resemblance alone is weak evidence. Many unrelated objects can look alike once blurred, overexposed or reduced to a few pixels. A more robust identification explains several observations at once: why the alleged UFO has its particular shape, why it occupies that location in the frame, why it changes position between images, and why a corresponding object or light exists inside the vehicle.

The best reflection cases therefore have a reproducible source. The BUFORA Peugeot example is useful because the investigator deliberately produced the photographs and could identify the steering-wheel emblem responsible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/post/reflecting-on-ufo-images" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">reflecting on ufo images</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">REFLECTING ON UFO IMAGESMarch 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 4, 2025</span></span></span> A comparable investigation of an unsolicited UFO photograph would ideally establish the vehicle model, recover uncropped original files, inspect adjacent photographs and recreate the image from the original passenger or driver's position.

By contrast, saying that an unexplained blob "could be a dashboard reflection" should remain a hypothesis when the vehicle interior, original files or shooting circumstances are unavailable. Single-image reflection separation is difficult enough to remain an active computer-vision research problem; researchers describe it as ill-posed because the observed image contains intertwined information from both the transmitted and reflected scenes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/single-image-reflection-removal-through-cascaded-refinement/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: microsoft.com">[Microsoft]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">microsoft.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Single Image Reflection Removal through Cascaded Refinement</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Single Image Reflection Removal through Cascaded Refinement - Microsoft ResearchJune 1, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2020</span></span></span> A low-resolution social-media copy provides even less evidence.

That distinction matters in UFO investigation. Recognising car-window reflections is not a licence to dismiss every photograph taken from a vehicle. It is a testable alternative explanation. When a suspected craft reproduces the geometry of a steering-wheel badge, cup holder, seat-belt fitting or illuminated control—and its position changes as reflection geometry predicts—the apparently distant UFO can be explained without requiring any object to have been in the sky at all.
