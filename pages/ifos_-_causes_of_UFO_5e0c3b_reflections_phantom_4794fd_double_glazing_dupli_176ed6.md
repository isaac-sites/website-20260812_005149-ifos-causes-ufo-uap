## Introduction

Double glazing can turn one ordinary light into two or more apparently separate lights. The effect occurs because an insulated window contains several partially reflective glass surfaces. Light from a lamp or other bright source on the observer’s side can reflect from more than one of those surfaces, and the resulting images need not fall in exactly the same place. Viewed against a dark sky, the duplicates can resemble a pair or formation of luminous objects rather than a window reflection.

This is not merely a theoretical possibility. Optical researchers use these displaced duplicates — usually called *ghosting* — as a measurable signature of [reflections]({{ 'reflections/' | relative_url }}) in photographs taken through glass. The glazing industry likewise recognises multiple reflected images as a normal physical property of insulating glass units, particularly noticeable against dark backgrounds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cv-foundation.org/openaccess/content_cvpr_2015/html/Shih_Reflection_Removal_Using_2015_CVPR_paper.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cv-foundation.org">[cv-foundation.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cv-foundation.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shih Reflection Removal Using 2015 CVPR paper</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CV FoundationCVPR 2015 Open Access Repository...</span></span></span>

For UFO or UAP investigation, that gives an important diagnostic clue: several lights with nearly identical shapes and spacing do not necessarily require several objects in the sky. Sometimes the apparent formation begins with a single source in the room.

## How several panes make several images

Glass is transparent, but it is not perfectly non-reflective. At an uncoated air-glass boundary, roughly 4 per cent of normally incident visible light is reflected; the exact amount depends on factors including refractive index, wavelength, coatings and viewing angle. A conventional double-glazed unit introduces several interfaces rather than one simple transparent boundary, giving light additional opportunities to be reflected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.edmundoptics.co.uk/knowledge-center/application-notes/lasers/an-introduction-to-optical-coatings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmundoptics.co.uk">[Edmund Optics]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmundoptics.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Edmund OpticsAn Introduction to Optical Coatings &#124; Edmund Optics...</span></span></span>

The important point is not simply that double glazing reflects *more* light. It is that reflections arising at physically separated surfaces can follow slightly different paths to the observer or camera.

A 2015 computer-vision study by YiChang Shih, Dilip Krishnan, Frédo Durand and William Freeman described this effect explicitly. In a double-pane window, the pane nearer the camera produces one reflected image while the other pane produces another, shifted image. The researchers called these displaced copies “ghosting cues” and developed a method for detecting them so that unwanted window reflections could be separated from the scene photographed through the glass.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://people.csail.mit.edu/yichangshih/mywebsite/reflection.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: people.csail.mit.edu">[MIT CSAIL]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">people.csail.mit.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CSAILReﬂection Removal using Ghosting Cues</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MIT CSAILReﬂection Removal using Ghosting CuesApril 13, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 13, 2015</span></span></span>

That work is particularly useful for understanding mysterious-light photographs because it treats the duplication as information rather than random photographic noise. If an indoor light is reflected into a photograph, the glazing itself can leave a characteristic structure: a brighter reflection accompanied by a displaced, often weaker copy.

The glass industry describes essentially the same phenomenon from a different perspective. Guidance on the visual quality of insulating glass units states that each pane can produce reflected images and that distortion or reflection effects multiply as panes are added. Triple glazing can enhance them further. Multiple reflections are therefore an inherent optical consequence of multi-pane construction rather than evidence that the window is defective.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://glassservices.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/GGF-IGU-VISUAL-QUALITY-STANDARD.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: glassservices.co.uk">[Glass Services]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">glassservices.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Glass Services SECTION4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Products, Glazing Techniques and Maintena...</span></span></span>

## Why the duplicates can resemble a UFO formation

The effect becomes especially deceptive when the reflected source is compact and bright. A ceiling spotlight, reading lamp or other small illuminated fitting does not necessarily produce an obvious picture of a lamp in the glass. Against a nearly black exterior it may contribute only its brightest feature: a dot, oval, short luminous bar or disc-like patch.

Double glazing can then repeat that simple shape.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Double_glazing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cameo.mfa.org">[cameo.mfa.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cameo.mfa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Double glazing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Double glazing</span></span></span>

Instead of thinking “one lamp plus its ghost”, an observer who does not realise that glass is involved may see “two identical lights”. Add further reflections, or additional bright sources in the room, and the result can become a line, pair or small cluster that appears organised. Technical glazing guidance specifically notes that multiple reflections become particularly conspicuous when the background seen through the window is dark — precisely the condition encountered when photographing the night sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/702136081/EN-1279-1-2018-EN" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EN 1279-1: Insulating Glass Standards &#124; PDF &#124; Glasses &#124; AdsorptionJuly 1, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1, 2018</span></span></span>

Oblique viewing makes the effect still more useful diagnostically. When the observer looks through glazing at an angle, rays reflected from separated surfaces can reach the camera along measurably displaced paths. Industry guidance notes that multiple reflected images are more apparent when glazing is viewed at an angle, while the computer-vision research models the reflected layer as shifted and attenuated copies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://islandglass.com.fj/technical-info/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: islandglass.com.fj">[Island Glass]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">islandglass.com.fj</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Island Glass Technical Info – Island Glass &amp; Mirror</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Island Glass Technical Info – Island Glass &amp; Mirror</span></span></span>

The duplicates also need not have precisely equal [brightness]({{ 'brightness/' | relative_url }}). Every additional reflection or transmission through glass changes the amount of light reaching the camera. Consequently, a pattern containing one strong “object” and one weaker companion can still originate from a single source. Optical systems with multiple reflecting surfaces are well known to produce such ghost images, which is one reason anti-reflection coatings are used to suppress them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.photonicsonline.com/doc/anti-reflection-coatings-0001" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: photonicsonline.com">[Photonics Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">photonicsonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Photonics Online Anti-Reflective (AR) Glass &amp; Coatings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Photonics Online Anti-Reflective (AR) Glass &amp; Coatings</span></span></span>

Nor should an investigator expect perfectly regular geometry in every real window. Insulating glass units can flex because of temperature and atmospheric-pressure differences, changing the reflected image. The Glass and Glazing Federation’s visual-quality guidance also notes that multiple panes multiply reflection and distortion effects. A pair of ghosts therefore need not behave like two immaculate copies produced by an ideal laboratory plate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://islandglass.com.fj/technical-info/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: islandglass.com.fj">[Island Glass]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">islandglass.com.fj</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Island Glass Technical Info – Island Glass &amp; Mirror</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Island Glass Technical Info – Island Glass &amp; Mirror</span></span></span>

## The Loch Ness photograph shows the mechanism in practice

A useful UFO-related example emerged in Scotland in 2015, when a holiday photograph overlooking Loch Ness appeared to contain two luminous, elongated objects in the sky. The picture attracted attention as a possible UFO photograph, but investigator Mick West argued that the objects were reflections of an indoor reading light, duplicated by the double-glazed window.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/debunked-ufo-over-loch-ness-reflection-in-dual-pane-window.6373/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Debunked: UFO over Loch Ness [Reflection in Dual Pane Window] &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Debunked: UFO over Loch Ness [Reflection in Dual Pane Window] &#124; Metabunk</span></span></span>

The value of this case is not simply that somebody proposed “reflection” as an alternative explanation. The location allowed the hypothesis to be tested.

West identified the viewpoint as Bunloit Farmhouse and contacted the property owner. The owner confirmed that the relevant windows were double glazed. She then photographed the view again from the corresponding indoor position while deliberately producing a reflection from the room light. The resulting photographs reproduced the characteristic shape, colour and doubled appearance of the supposed objects. A reflected curtain visible in the original image also supported the conclusion that glass lay between the camera and the landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/debunked-ufo-over-loch-ness-reflection-in-dual-pane-window.6373/post-160622" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on metabunk.org.</span></span></span>

There was a dispute over the circumstances: the original witnesses maintained that their photograph had been taken outside, whereas the reflection analysis placed the camera indoors. Contemporary coverage recorded that disagreement. The recreation is nevertheless significant because it demonstrated experimentally that the glazing and room lighting at the actual location could generate the distinctive paired “UFO” pattern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.inquisitr.com/controversy-rages-over-loch-ness-ufo-image/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: inquisitr.com">[Inquisitr News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">inquisitr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Controversy Rages Over Loch Ness UFO Image</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">News Controversy Rages Over Loch Ness UFO Image</span></span></span>

The case therefore illustrates a broader investigative lesson. A photograph containing *two* anomalous objects can initially seem harder to explain than one: two similar objects suggest formation flying, coordination or a common aircraft design. With double glazing, however, similarity between the objects can point in exactly the opposite direction. The second object may be evidence that the first is a reflection too.

## Reflection patterns investigators can compare

Double-glazing ghosting has several features that can be checked against an unexplained photograph. None is decisive by itself, but several occurring together can make the reflection hypothesis substantially stronger.

* **Near-identical shapes.** Two lights derived from the same source should reproduce much of the same structure. A peculiar oval, bar or irregular bright patch appearing twice is therefore more informative than two unrelated points.
* **Systematic displacement.** The copies are normally offset rather than randomly scattered. Optical research specifically models window ghosting as shifted reflection components.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cv-foundation.org/openaccess/content_cvpr_2015/html/Shih_Reflection_Removal_Using_2015_CVPR_paper.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cv-foundation.org">[CV Foundation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cv-foundation.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shih Reflection Removal Using 2015 CVPR paper</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CV FoundationCVPR 2015 Open Access Repository...</span></span></span>
* **Unequal intensity.** One copy may be fainter because the optical paths involve different combinations of transmission and reflection.
* **A dark exterior.** Multiple reflections are easiest to notice against dark backgrounds, making night photographs especially susceptible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skpstiklas.lt/uploads/Vizualine_stiklo_paketu_kokybe--2025EN.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skpstiklas.lt">[Skp stiklas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skpstiklas.lt</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skp stiklas Page: 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I. * **An oblique camera angle.** Looking diagonally through the panes tends to make separated reflected images easier to distinguish.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://islandglass.com.fj/technical-info/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: islandglass.com.fj">[Island Glass]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">islandglass.com.fj</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Island Glass Technical Info – Island Glass &amp; Mirror</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Island Glass Technical Info – Island Glass &amp; Mirror</span></span></span>
* **Other reflected room details.** Curtains, furniture, illuminated controls or faint interior shapes elsewhere in the frame can establish that the window was acting as a partial mirror. In the Loch Ness example, the curtain reflection became an important contextual clue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/debunked-ufo-over-loch-ness-reflection-in-dual-pane-window.6373/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Debunked: UFO over Loch Ness [Reflection in Dual Pane Window] &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Debunked: UFO over Loch Ness [Reflection in Dual Pane Window] &#124; Metabunk</span></span></span>
* **Repeatability.** If the original location is accessible, placing a bright source on the camera side of the same glazing and changing the camera angle can be more persuasive than arguing from appearance alone. The Loch Ness recreation is a particularly clear example of this approach.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/debunked-ufo-over-loch-ness-reflection-in-dual-pane-window.6373/post-160622" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on metabunk.org.</span></span></span>

Movement provides another potentially strong test when video is available. A genuine distant formation has to maintain its geometry according to the motion of objects in three-dimensional space. Reflections are tied instead to the geometry connecting the source, panes and camera. Moving the camera sideways or changing its angle can therefore shift the ghosts in ways characteristic of the window rather than the distant landscape.

This is closely related to why ghosting is useful in computational photography. The 2015 CVPR research could distinguish the unwanted reflection layer partly because its duplicated structure behaved differently from the scene transmitted through the glass. What is an undesirable artefact to a photographer can thus become useful evidence to an investigator.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2015/html/Shih_Reflection_Removal_Using_2015_CVPR_paper.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openaccess.thecvf.com">[CVF Open Access]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openaccess.thecvf.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shih Reflection Removal Using 2015 CVPR paper</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CVF Open AccessCVPR 2015 Open Access Repository...</span></span></span>

## When duplication is strong evidence — and when it is not

Finding two similar lights does not automatically prove double glazing. Aircraft can genuinely fly in formation, distant lamps can resemble one another, and other optical systems can also generate duplicate images. Even a single pane of sufficiently thick glass can create displaced reflections from its two surfaces; Shih and colleagues explicitly observed ghosting from both single- and double-pane glass.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cv-foundation.org/openaccess/content_cvpr_2015/html/Shih_Reflection_Removal_Using_2015_CVPR_paper.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cv-foundation.org">[CV Foundation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cv-foundation.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shih Reflection Removal Using 2015 CVPR paper</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CV FoundationCVPR 2015 Open Access Repository...</span></span></span>

The stronger conclusion comes from matching several independent features at once: confirmation that the photograph was taken through multi-pane glazing; a plausible bright source on the observer’s side; duplicates with corresponding shape and brightness structure; displacement compatible with the window geometry; and, ideally, reproduction of the effect from the original viewpoint.

Conversely, if no glass intervened between observer and sky, this particular explanation fails. The same applies if reconstructed reflections cannot reproduce the reported geometry or if independent observers outside the building recorded the same lights from different positions. “Double glazing” should therefore be a testable hypothesis, not a generic label applied to every mysterious cluster.

What makes the mechanism especially valuable in UFO and UAP analysis is that the very feature that initially seems extraordinary — several matching lights appearing together — can provide the clue to a mundane origin. Modern insulated glazing contains multiple partially reflective surfaces, glazing standards acknowledge the resulting multiple images, and computer-vision researchers can mathematically exploit their characteristic displacement. In the right circumstances, a supposed formation of objects in the night sky is therefore better understood as one nearby light, copied by the window in front of the camera.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://glassservices.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/GGF-IGU-VISUAL-QUALITY-STANDARD.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: glassservices.co.uk">[Glass Services]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">glassservices.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Glass Services SECTION4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Products, Glazing Techniques and Maintena...</span></span></span>
