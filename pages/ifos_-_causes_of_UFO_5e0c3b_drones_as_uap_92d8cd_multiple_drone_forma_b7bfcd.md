## Introduction

Several [drones]({{ 'drones/' | relative_url }}) flying together can produce one of the more convincing modern “UFO” impressions: a line, triangle, V-shape or compact cluster of lights that appears to belong to a single large craft. The effect does not require speculative technology. Coordinated drone systems already place hundreds or thousands of illuminated aircraft into precisely controlled geometric patterns, while much smaller groups can maintain [formations]({{ 'formations/' | relative_url }}) for ordinary operational purposes. At night, the crucial visual cue is that observers may see the lights but not the separate airframes supporting them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.intel.co.uk/content/dam/www/public/us/en/documents/fact-sheets/intel-drone-light-show-fact-sheet.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intel.co.uk">[Intel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intel.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intel Intel Drone Light Show Classic Fact Sheet</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intel Drone Light Show Classic Fact Sheet &#124; --- &#124; ---November 6, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 6, 2020</span></span></span>

That distinction matters when assessing UFO/UAP reports. Three lights maintaining apparently rigid triangular spacing do not, by themselves, demonstrate a triangular vehicle connecting them. They establish three visible light sources whose distances and three-dimensional arrangement may be poorly constrained. Real-world drone operations show that multiple aircraft can be coordinated closely enough to make the alternative explanation physically credible; perception and viewing geometry can then make a formation look more rigid, larger or stranger than it really is.

## How separate drones form lines, triangles and clusters

Modern multi-drone flight is capable of far more coordination than is obvious when looking at a handful of lights in a dark sky. Purpose-built light-show drones effectively function as independently positioned illuminated pixels. An<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.intel.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intel.com">[Intel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on intel.com.</span></span></span> technical fact sheet described its Shooting Star system as using hundreds or thousands of pre-programmed drones, with one computer controlling the entire fleet. The aircraft carried programmable LEDs, while software automated the construction of aerial shapes and animations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.intel.co.uk/content/dam/www/public/us/en/documents/fact-sheets/intel-drone-light-show-fact-sheet.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intel.co.uk">[Intel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intel.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intel Intel Drone Light Show Classic Fact Sheet</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intel Drone Light Show Classic Fact Sheet &#124; --- &#124; ---November 6, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 6, 2020</span></span></span>

The scale already demonstrated publicly makes a three-, five- or ten-light formation technologically unremarkable. At the 2018 PyeongChang Winter Olympics, 1,218 Shooting Star drones were flown simultaneously to produce recognisable moving figures and the Olympic rings. Later that year, 2,018 drones flew together over Folsom, California. The latter fleet was controlled by one pilot rather than thousands of operators independently trying to maintain position.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/172/intel-drone-light-show-breaks-guinness-world-records-title" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intc.com">[Intel Corporation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intel drone light show breaks guinness world records title</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intel CorporationIntel Drone Light Show Breaks Guinness World Records Title at Olympic Winter Games PyeongChang 2018:: Intel Corporation...</span></span></span>

Current systems can achieve still tighter positioning. Drone-show operator Firefly says its show aircraft use real-time kinematic satellite positioning, or RTK, with correction data distributed across the fleet to obtain centimetre-level positioning and maintain tight formations. Recent robotics research likewise treats large drone displays as a problem of assigning individual aircraft to precise positions while calculating collision-free paths between successive formations. A 2026 study demonstrated trajectory planning in simulation for formations involving more than 1,000 drones.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fireflydroneshows.com/our-gen2-drone" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fireflydroneshows.com">[Firefly Drone Shows]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fireflydroneshows.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Firefly Drone Shows Firefly Gen2 Drone &#124; Purpose-Built Drone Light Show Hardware</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Firefly Drone Shows Firefly Gen2 Drone &#124; Purpose-Built Drone Light Show Hardware</span></span></span>

For UFO interpretation, the important point is not that a mysterious triangle must therefore be a commercial drone show. Most unidentified sightings plainly are not choreographed entertainment. The demonstrations establish a narrower fact: **separate small aircraft can hold geometrically organised positions and move together closely enough to create the visual impression of a coherent aerial structure**.

A few illuminated drones could consequently reproduce several commonly reported configurations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A straight line:** aircraft maintain similar headings with approximately equal spacing.
* **A triangle:** three visible drones occupy the vertices while the dark space between them is interpreted as the body of an object.
* **A V or chevron:** several drones are staggered around a common direction of travel.
* **A compact cluster:** drones at different ranges or altitudes project into a much tighter apparent group from one viewing position.
* **A changing “craft”:** drones alter their relative positions, causing the apparent outline to stretch, rotate, split or reorganise even though each light belongs to a separate aircraft.

</div>

This is particularly significant at night because the eye may have little evidence that the dark area between lights is empty. The perceived object can become the *shape implied by the lights*, rather than the collection of small vehicles actually producing them.

## Why precise spacing can look like one solid object

Perspective collapses a three-dimensional arrangement into the two-dimensional angular pattern seen by an observer or camera. Two drones do not have to be physically side by side to appear so; one may be substantially farther away. Likewise, three aircraft at different ranges and heights can temporarily project into an apparently clean triangle.

That ambiguity becomes much harder to resolve when only lights are visible. A witness ordinarily cannot obtain physical dimensions from angular separation alone. Suppose three lights span several degrees of sky: without knowing their [distance]({{ 'distance/' | relative_url }}), that angle cannot establish whether they mark a modest formation of nearby drones or the corners of an enormous distant object. NASA's UAP study emphasised the wider importance of calibrated, multi-sensor observations precisely because ordinary reports often lack the measurements needed for reliable scientific interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/update-nasa-shares-uap-independent-study-report-names-director/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UPDATE: NASA Shares UAP Independent Study Report; Names Director - NASASeptember 14, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 14, 2023</span></span></span>

Motion can reinforce the illusion. If three lights preserve similar angular spacing while crossing the observer's [field of view]({{ 'field-of-view/' | relative_url }}), the visual system has a plausible reason to group them. The result can resemble navigation lights fixed to one dark vehicle. But formation flight produces much the same observational signature: independent aircraft following coordinated paths.

This gives investigators an important distinction to test. **Constant angular spacing is not the same thing as evidence of a connecting structure.** Stronger evidence for one physical object would include a consistently resolved silhouette, occultation of background stars, [radar]({{ 'radar/' | relative_url }}) or other ranging data showing the lights at the same distance, or imagery resolving a continuous body between them. Conversely, visible changes in relative spacing, individual lights departing independently, or measurements placing the lights at different ranges would favour a multiple-object interpretation.

The viewing location matters as well. A formation that appears beautifully symmetric from one direction can look elongated or disordered from another. That is why videos taken from substantially separated positions can be disproportionately valuable: they can reveal [parallax]({{ 'parallax/' | relative_url }}) — the change in apparent position caused by observing the same objects from different locations — and help reconstruct whether the lights occupy one plane, one distance, or several independent positions.

## Real operations show that multi-drone incidents can persist

Large entertainment displays prove what coordinated systems can achieve under controlled conditions, but prolonged real-world incidents demonstrate a different point: multiple drones can also operate repeatedly in the same area without forming an advertised spectacle.

In December 2023, Joint Base Langley-Eustis in Virginia experienced repeated incursions by unauthorised unmanned aerial systems. Official accounts later confirmed that the number of aircraft fluctuated and that multiple incursions occurred during the month. The episode became significant enough to influence military operations and subsequent discussion of counter-drone capabilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/committees/testimony/pdf/?apn=b2025_26%2Fyear1%2Fother_committees%2Fctte_jt_ks_security_1%2Ftestimony%2Fpublished%2Fctte_jt_ks_security_1_20251024_01_testimony.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kslegislature.gov">[Kansas Legislature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kslegislature.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kansas Legislature Kansas Adjutant General’s Department</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kansas LegislatureKansas Adjutant General’s DepartmentOctober 22, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 22, 2025</span></span></span>

That episode should not be stretched beyond the evidence: identifying objects as UAS does not establish who operated them, what every individual sighting represented, or that they deliberately attempted to resemble UFOs. Its relevance here is operational. A prolonged series of observations involving multiple small aircraft is not inherently implausible simply because maintaining repeated nighttime activity sounds too complicated for ordinary drones.

The much more public [New Jersey]({{ 'new-jersey/' | relative_url }}) sighting wave of late 2024 illustrates the additional difficulty created when genuine drone activity, ordinary aircraft and ambiguous observations become mixed together. On 3 December, the FBI said witnesses had reported a **cluster** of apparent drones and a possible fixed-wing aircraft along the Raritan River. Nine days later, the FBI and Department of Homeland Security cautioned that many submitted images appeared instead to show lawfully operating manned aircraft and that electronic detection had not corroborated the reported visual sightings then under review.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/newark/news/fbi-newark-seeks-information-on-drone-sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">newark seeks information on drone sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">newark seeks information on drone sightings</span></span></span>

By 17 December, the FBI, Department of Homeland Security, Federal Aviation Administration and Department of Defense said more than 5,000 tips had produced roughly 100 investigative leads. Their assessment was not that every report had one explanation: the observations included lawful commercial, hobbyist and law-enforcement drones, manned aeroplanes, helicopters and stars mistakenly reported as drones. They said they had found nothing anomalous in the activity assessed to that point.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/news/press-releases/dhs-fbi-faa-and-dod-joint-statement-on-ongoing-response-to-reported-drone-sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on fbi.gov.</span></span></span>

This is a useful caution for formation sightings. A group of lights reported as “drones” does not automatically prove that all of them really are drones, just as a group reported as a “UFO” does not prove that the lights belong to one craft. In a busy night sky, several independent categories can overlap in the same account.

## Coordination is possible without a pilot for every light

One intuitive objection to the multi-drone explanation is that keeping numerous aircraft in precise formation would require an implausibly large team of expert pilots. Existing technology shows why that assumption is outdated.

The FAA's rules actually recognise the possibility of one remote pilot operating multiple small unmanned aircraft: doing so under US Part 107 requires a waiver from the normal rule governing operation of multiple small UAS. The FAA explicitly lists “fly multiple small UAS with only one remote pilot” among operations for which a waiver may be sought.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/part_107_waivers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on faa.gov.</span></span></span>

Automation makes much larger fleets possible. In light shows, individual flight paths can be computed in advance and executed as a synchronised programme rather than being manually flown second by second. Intel's system used a single computer to control an entire fleet, while modern research focuses on automatically assigning hundreds or thousands of aircraft to successive points in a formation and calculating routes that prevent collisions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.intel.co.uk/content/dam/www/public/us/en/documents/fact-sheets/intel-drone-light-show-fact-sheet.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intel.co.uk">[Intel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intel.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intel Intel Drone Light Show Classic Fact Sheet</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intel Drone Light Show Classic Fact Sheet &#124; --- &#124; ---November 6, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 6, 2020</span></span></span>

This changes what should count as “too coordinated to be drones”. Smooth collective movement, equal-looking spacing or simultaneous changes of direction may be striking, but none is beyond demonstrated multi-drone capability. Even complex three-dimensional figures have been deliberately generated using fleets of illuminated quadcopters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/172/intel-drone-light-show-breaks-guinness-world-records-title" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intc.com">[Intel Corporation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intel drone light show breaks guinness world records title</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intel CorporationIntel Drone Light Show Breaks Guinness World Records Title at Olympic Winter Games PyeongChang 2018:: Intel Corporation...</span></span></span>

The evidential question is therefore not whether drones *could possibly* produce an organised pattern. They clearly can. The useful questions are whether drones fit the particular incident's duration, altitude, speed, lighting, sound, airspace, weather and recorded trajectories — and whether independent observations reveal several aircraft rather than one.

## What makes a formation sighting genuinely harder to explain

A drone-formation hypothesis is strongest when the observation consists mainly of unresolved lights moving at plausible low-altitude aircraft speeds, especially when the lights change their spacing or separate independently. It becomes weaker when good-quality evidence establishes behaviour incompatible with the performance or geometry of the proposed drones.

The distinction is important because nighttime videos often contain far less information than witnesses understandably feel they contain. A sharp triangle of three lights may look structurally meaningful while providing no direct image of a triangular structure. Conversely, investigators should not dismiss a report merely because “three drones could make a triangle”. That is a hypothesis to test, not an identification.

Useful discriminating evidence includes simultaneous views from separated locations, original video files with timestamps and metadata, known camera direction and field of view, radar or other range measurements, [flight-tracking]({{ 'flight-tracking/' | relative_url }}) information where applicable, and long recordings that capture how the formation arrives, changes and departs. Those data can turn an impressive visual pattern into measurable trajectories.

The broader lesson for UFO/UAP analysis is therefore modest but important. **Geometry alone is weak evidence for a single structured craft.** Multiple drones have demonstrably reached a level of coordination at which lines, rings, figures and intricate three-dimensional formations can be deliberately produced, while ordinary perspective can make a much smaller group appear rigid and unified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/142/intel-breaks-guinness-world-records-title-for-drone-light" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intc.com">[Intel Corporation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on intc.com.</span></span></span>

When several lights appear to outline one enormous dark object, the first analytical task is to establish whether there is actually an object between those lights. Until independent range, imaging or other sensor evidence answers that question, a formation of separate drones remains a technically established — and sometimes visually convincing — alternative.
