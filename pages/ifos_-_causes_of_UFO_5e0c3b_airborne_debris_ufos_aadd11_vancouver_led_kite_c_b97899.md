## Introduction

In February 2011, residents of Vancouver, Washington, reported an unusual display over the Fisher’s Landing area: bright, multicoloured lights that appeared after dark, hovered or moved around the sky, and seemed to “dance” back and forth. Some witnesses interpreted the illuminated shape as a flying saucer. The explanation, established when the operator later demonstrated it for local journalists, was much more terrestrial: a very large triangular kite covered with hundreds of programmable LED lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.heraldnet.com/2011/03/09/ufo-nope-just-a-kite-with-lights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heraldnet.com">[HeraldNet.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heraldnet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Herald Net.com UF O? ## What witnesses saw over Vancouver

The sightings were concentrated around Fisher’s Landing in east Vancouver. Contemporary reports described something visible only after dark, high in the sky, moving strangely back and forth while producing vivid sequences of colour. Some observers compared it with a saucer; the sightings attracted enough attention to spread beyond Vancouver into the wider regional media.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2011/mar/10/giant-kite-lights-night-sky-ufo-fans-imaginations/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spokesman.com">[Spokesman-Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spokesman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Review Giant kite lights night sky, UFO fans’ imaginations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ReviewGiant kite lights night sky, UFO fans’ imaginations...</span></span></span>

Archived UFO-report data add useful detail about what people thought they were seeing on 20 February. Entries for Vancouver at around 19:00–19:45 described a bright object hovering for several minutes, a glowing red sphere and an object showing green and red lights. One report classified the object simply as a “Light”, while another classified it as a “Disk”. These labels are important because they show how the same general display could produce different apparent shapes when observers were primarily seeing illumination rather than a clearly resolved physical body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://puretranquilitypublishing.com/ufo-sightings-2011/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: puretranquilitypublishing.com">[Pure Tranquility Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">puretranquilitypublishing.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo sightings 2011</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pure Tranquility PublishingUFO Sightings 2011 - Pure Tranquility Publishing...</span></span></span>

The range of explanations proposed publicly was similarly broad. Contemporary coverage recorded suggestions including [fireworks]({{ 'fireworks/' | relative_url }}), Jupiter, the aurora, a radio-controlled helicopter, helicopter training and a military drone. Others insisted that the apparent object was too large to be a kite or balloon. At the same time, some commenters correctly suggested an LED kite before the owner publicly demonstrated it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.heraldnet.com/2011/03/09/ufo-nope-just-a-kite-with-lights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heraldnet.com">[HeraldNet.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heraldnet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Herald Net.com UF O? This variety is itself revealing. Without a dependable estimate of range, altitude or physical dimensions, witnesses were trying to infer the identity of an unseen object from its lights and motion. “Disk”, “sphere” and “light” were therefore interpretations of essentially the same limited night-time information rather than measurements of a solid craft's geometry.

## How the illuminated kite was identified

The decisive evidence arrived in early March. The kite's owner, identified in contemporary reporting only as Mike, realised after seeing news coverage that people had been interpreting his night flying as a UFO. He invited a team from *The Columbian* to watch him reproduce the display at Clearmeadows Park in Fisher’s Landing. The City of Vancouver identifies Clearmeadows as a 5.7-acre neighbourhood park with an open lawn in Fisher’s Landing East, making it a plausible launch area for a substantial kite.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.heraldnet.com/2011/03/09/ufo-nope-just-a-kite-with-lights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heraldnet.com">[HeraldNet.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heraldnet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Herald Net.com UF O? Nope, just a kite with lights &#124; Herald Net.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO? Nope, just a kite with lights &#124; HeraldNet.comMarch 9, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2011</span></span></span>

This was no small children's kite. Contemporary descriptions give its dimensions as about 7½ feet (2.3 metres) tall with a 13-foot (4-metre) wingspan. It was triangular, made from parachute fabric and manufactured in China. Its numerous LEDs were powered by an onboard rechargeable lithium-ion battery and controlled by a small computer that generated changing sequences rather than simply leaving the lights continuously illuminated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2011/mar/09/nw-today-vancouver-ufo-identified-kite-lights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spokesman.com">[Spokesman-Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spokesman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Review NW today: Vancouver UFO identified as kite with lights</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ReviewNW today: Vancouver UFO identified as kite with lights...</span></span></span>

Those sequences could be elaborate. Reporting from the demonstration described gold-and-green illumination resembling a palm tree changing into an upward-moving red display. The programme ran through roughly five minutes of patterns before repeating. ## Why the lights looked like a craft

The Vancouver case illustrates a particularly effective night-time illusion: **the observer sees the illumination but loses the structure that explains it**.

In daylight, a four-metre triangular kite, a long tail and a line running towards the ground provide abundant clues about what is being observed. At night, the dark parachute fabric and thin tether can become difficult or impossible to resolve against the sky. The LEDs remain conspicuous because they generate their own light. The perceptual problem is then reversed: instead of seeing a kite decorated with lights, the witness sees lights and has to infer what invisible object connects them.

The programmed patterns made that ambiguity stronger. Hundreds of LEDs could illuminate different parts of the kite and tail in sequence, changing the apparent outline as different lamps switched on and off. A witness might consequently perceive a disk, sphere, elongated display or changing shape even though the underlying kite retained the same physical geometry. Contemporary descriptions of the Vancouver sightings as a light, disk or sphere are consistent with precisely this loss of structural information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://puretranquilitypublishing.com/ufo-sightings-2011/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: puretranquilitypublishing.com">[Pure Tranquility Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">puretranquilitypublishing.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo sightings 2011</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pure Tranquility PublishingUFO Sightings 2011 - Pure Tranquility Publishing...</span></span></span>

The tether also helps explain the apparently unusual motion. A kite can remain within one portion of the sky rather than continuously travelling through it like an aeroplane, yet it can still swing, surge and move sideways as wind and line tension vary. During the Vancouver episode, witnesses specifically described the illuminated object as hovering or dancing back and forth. Those behaviours seemed anomalous when attributed to a free-flying craft but were readily reproduced by the tethered kite.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2011/mar/09/nw-today-vancouver-ufo-identified-kite-lights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spokesman.com">[Spokesman-Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spokesman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Review NW today: Vancouver UFO identified as kite with lights</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ReviewNW today: Vancouver UFO identified as kite with lights...</span></span></span>

The operator's equipment also shows why judging the display as “too big to be a kite” from the lights alone was unreliable. The actual kite already had a 13-foot wingspan, far larger than the small recreational kite many observers may have pictured when considering that explanation. Its illuminated tail could extend another 60 feet. Without a known [distance]({{ 'distance/' | relative_url }}) or visible tether, there was no straightforward visual scale against which a night-time observer could estimate its real dimensions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.heraldnet.com/2011/03/09/ufo-nope-just-a-kite-with-lights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heraldnet.com">[HeraldNet.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heraldnet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Herald Net.com UF O? ## The identification was stronger than a visual resemblance

Many explanations of UFO photographs amount to resemblance: an image *looks like* a balloon, bird, aircraft or kite. Vancouver offered a more informative chain of evidence.

The reported phenomenon occurred around Fisher’s Landing; an identifiable local operator acknowledged flying a large illuminated kite there; the kite's dimensions and hundreds of programmable LEDs accounted for the conspicuous colours and apparent shape; and the owner then reproduced the display for journalists at Clearmeadows Park. The same demonstration showed directly how a tethered object could move around the sky while its lighting programme repeatedly changed its appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.heraldnet.com/2011/03/09/ufo-nope-just-a-kite-with-lights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heraldnet.com">[HeraldNet.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heraldnet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Herald Net.com UF O? There is also a useful distinction between identification and accusations of deliberate deception. Some later summaries have called the episode a “hoax”, but the stronger contemporary reporting does not establish that Mike originally flew the kite in order to manufacture UFO reports. Instead, it says that after discovering that people had misinterpreted his kite, he contacted journalists and demonstrated it. He also said he had discussed his kite flying with an FAA official and Vancouver police, and he warned emergency dispatch before the demonstration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.heraldnet.com/2011/03/09/ufo-nope-just-a-kite-with-lights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heraldnet.com">[HeraldNet.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heraldnet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Herald Net.com UF O? That makes **misidentification** the more evidence-based description of the case. The UFO-like appearance was a consequence of an unusual illuminated recreational object being observed without the daylight cues that would normally reveal its identity.

The aviation detail also provides a useful check on exaggerated claims surrounding the event. Contemporary coverage reported that Mike said he kept the kite below 500 feet. ## What the Vancouver kite case demonstrates

The Vancouver incident is a compact example of why unusual night-time lights should not automatically be treated as lights mounted on an aircraft. In this case, nearly every striking feature had a mundane source: hovering came from a tethered kite, erratic lateral movement from its response to the wind, dramatic colour changes from computer-controlled LEDs, and the absence of an obvious body from dark fabric viewed against a night sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.heraldnet.com/2011/03/09/ufo-nope-just-a-kite-with-lights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heraldnet.com">[HeraldNet.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heraldnet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Herald Net.com UF O? Nope, just a kite with lights &#124; HeraldNet.comMarch 9, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2011</span></span></span>

Most importantly, the identification did not require dismissing what witnesses reported seeing. People really did see a large and unusual illuminated object moving above Vancouver. The error lay in interpreting the visible lights as evidence for the size, shape and propulsion of an otherwise unseen craft. Once the kite was observed on the ground and then relaunched, those apparently extraordinary characteristics became properties of an elaborate recreational light display.

For the broader problem of kites and airborne objects mistaken for UFOs, Vancouver therefore supplies an unusually clear diagnostic lesson: when an object is reported mainly as **lights that hover, sway, change colour or alter apparent shape after dark**, investigators should consider whether the luminous pattern is revealing the object itself or concealing it. In Fisher’s Landing in 2011, the spectacular lights that made the object seem mysterious were also the very equipment that ultimately explained it.
